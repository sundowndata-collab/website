Why private matters

Why a Kenya safari for photographers needs your own vehicle

Most safaris tolerate photographers. On a shared game drive the vehicle leaves when the group is bored, the engine runs while you shoot, and the best side of the vehicle belongs to whoever sat there first.

A private wildlife photo safari flips that. You decide where the vehicle stops, which side faces the subject and how long you wait for the moment.

Shared game drive

  • Engine running, vibration in the frame
  • Fixed seat, fixed angle

Your photo safari

  • You stay until the light is right
  • Engine off for a steady shot

When to go

There is no bad month, only different pictures. Pick the season by the shot you want to bring home.

Dry and clear

Clean skies, short grass for big cat action and lower park fees. A quiet favourite for photographers.

Apr to May

Green and moody

June

The herds arrive

Still green, with the migration starting to move north into the Mara.

Jul to Oct

Migration and crossings

Mara River crossings, dust and backlit action. Peak fees and busy sightings, so book early.

Nov to Dec

Soft light

Short rains, fresh grass, newborns and softer crowds before the holidays.

Questions photographers ask

Kenya photography safari FAQ

What is the best Kenya safari for photographers?

A private safari with your own vehicle and guide, timed around the golden hours. In a shared vehicle the group decides when to leave a sighting. In a private one you stay until the light is right. For most photographers that means a few days in the Masai Mara, ideally with time in a private conservancy where off road driving lets you choose the angle. Our photo safaris are led by professional wildlife photographers who coach you in real time, and our 3 day Photographer and 5 day Horizon Chaser are built for exactly this.

What camera gear should I bring on a Kenya photography safari?

A telephoto zoom such as a 100 to 400mm or 200 to 600mm for wildlife, and a wide angle around 16 to 35mm for landscapes and skies. Bring a second body if you have one so you are not changing lenses in the dust, plus spare batteries, plenty of memory cards, a lens blower and a rain cover. Our Land Cruisers carry side window beanbag mounts and charging points, so you do not need to pack your own beanbag and can top up batteries between sightings.

When is the best time for a photography safari in Kenya?

July to October brings the migration and the Mara River crossings, with dust and dramatic action. January to March is dry and clear with lower park fees and excellent big cat sightings. April and May are green and quiet with moody skies. Because Kenya sits on the equator, golden hour falls at roughly the same time all year, around 6:30 in the morning and 6:45 in the evening.

Is a private vehicle really worth it for wildlife photography?

For photographers it is the single biggest difference. You choose which side of the vehicle faces the subject, your guide switches off the engine so there is no shake, and nobody asks to move on when the light is about to turn. In a private conservancy you can also leave the track to find a cleaner background.
